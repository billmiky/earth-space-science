Gaia Hypothesis Extra Credit: Teacher Key

Unit 2 · Coevolution of Earth and Life 5E · Thanksgiving break · 10 points extra credit

Open student extra credit Chapter link

Why it's hard to outsource to AI

  • Handwritten and hand-drawn only: graph, two diagrams and all answers. Typed work earns no credit.
  • Personal data AI can't know: a feedback loop the student actually saw at home (Part 4a) and a family interview with a named person and a quote (Part 4b).
  • Packet-anchored: evidence must come from the student's own Coevolution 5E work and a fixed evidence bank (E1–E8).
  • Return-day check (announced on the cover): as a Do Now, students redraw their Part 3 feedback loop from memory in 3 minutes with no notes, then explain it to a partner. Compare it to their written work. A big mismatch is a conversation, not an accusation.

No assignment is fully AI-proof. A student could copy an AI answer by hand. The return-day redraw and the personal interview are what make that not worth doing.

Part 1 · Reading (1 pt)

1a (0.5 pt). Same: life and Earth's systems change each other (coevolution). Further: Gaia says the combined system regulates itself and keeps conditions livable, mostly through negative feedback.

1b (0.5 pt). Use telescopes to read the gases in an exoplanet's atmosphere (spectroscopy). Finding gases that destroy each other, like O₂ and CH₄, together would suggest something keeps making them, possibly life (a biosignature).

Part 2 · Daisyworld (2 pts)

2a (0.5 pt). Both lines plotted correctly, dashed "no life" (straight-ish rising line), solid "with daisies" (jumps up at 0.8, flat around 20–25 °C, rejoins the bare line at 1.6). Key, axis labels, 5–40 °C band shaded.

2b (0.25 pt). No life: 59 − 11 = 48 °C. Daisies: highest 25 °C, lowest 20 °C, so 25 − 20 = 5 °C (accept 19.6 → 25.0 if they computed more precisely).

2c (0.5 pt). White daisies take over (12% → 70%); black daisies shrink (55% → 0%). White daisies have high albedo, so the planet reflects more sunlight as the sun brightens, canceling the extra heating. Early on, black daisies (low albedo) absorb more and warm the cold planet.

2d (0.25 pt). Negative feedback: a change (warming) causes a response (more white daisies) that pushes the temperature back toward where it was.

2e (0.25 pt). Each daisy type just grows best where the local temperature suits it. When it's too warm, white daisies (cooler patches) out-grow black ones; that shift cools the planet. Regulation emerges from growth and competition (selection), with no planning or purpose.

2f (0.25 pt). At 1.6 the planet is too hot, all daisies die and the temperature jumps to the no-life value (64 °C). Life can only buffer change up to a limit; a big enough push (like the volcanic CO₂ of the Permian extinction) overwhelms the feedback.

Data note: the table is model output (Watson & Lovelock 1983, Tellus B 35:284–289), computed from the published equations with standard parameters and rounded. It is labeled for students as a model, not real data.

Part 3 · Earth feedback loop (2 pts)

  • Full loop with ≥4 linked boxes and sketches (0.5) · sphere labels and correct sign (0.5) · real evidence (0.5) · 3b (0.5)
  • Loop A (weathering thermostat): atmosphere (warm, CO₂) → hydrosphere (rain) → geosphere (weathering; biosphere roots speed it) → atmosphere (CO₂ removed, cooling). Negative. Supports Gaia: it stabilizes climate. Evidence: plants spreading on land lowered CO₂.
  • Loop B (photosynthesis–oxygen): biosphere (cyanobacteria) → hydrosphere (BIFs) → atmosphere (O₂, ozone) → biosphere (eukaryotes, land life). Positive (amplifying). Best answer: both. It made Earth better for complex life, but it destabilized Earth (poisoned anaerobes, Huronian ice age), which challenges strong Gaia.

Part 4 · Gaia at home (2 pts)

4a (1 pt). Specific real observation and sketch with sensor, response and correct sign (0.5) · comparison (0.5). Strong answers notice that a thermostat or oven was designed by people with a set point, while Daisyworld and Earth have no designer; the body is a good middle case (built by natural selection). Most home loops are negative feedback; a pot boiling over or a microphone squeal is positive.

4b (1 pt). Named person and a believable quote (0.5) · response with one packet-based piece of evidence (0.5). Any opinion from the family member is fine; grade the student's evidence.
