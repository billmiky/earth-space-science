# Tracking Student Progress: Standards + Skills (Earth & Space Sciences)

Teacher-only resource. This folder holds:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | This guide: what to track, and ways to track ~100 students |
| `ESS-progress-tracker.xlsx` | A blank tracker template (opens in Excel or Google Sheets). Enter scores once and it fills in each student's standards and skills mastery automatically |
| `regents-item-map.csv` | All 250 questions from the **June 2025, Aug 2025, Jan 2026, June 2026, and Aug 2026** ESS Regents, each tagged with a standard, a skill, and its format |

> ⚠️ **Never put real student names or scores in this repo.** The whole repository is published as the public textbook site. Copy the template to your school Google Drive and keep student data there.

> The question tags are a **teacher-made alignment, not NYSED's official map**. Several items could fairly fit two standards (the second one is in `pe2`). Compare against NYSED's published standards map for each exam if you have it, and edit the Item Map sheet wherever you disagree.

---

## 1. What the five Regents exams actually test

All 250 questions are worth **1 credit**. Each exam has **50 items in 10–11 clusters**, and every cluster opens with a reading passage plus graphs, maps, or models.

- **63% multiple choice, 37% constructed response.** Every constructed response is 1 credit and short: one or two sentences, a check-box with a justification, filling in a table, or labeling a model. There was no graph plotting on any of the five exams. Only 5 items were calculations and 3 were drawings.
- **About 1 in 5 items (21%) needs the 2024 Reference Tables.** Solar System Data, Rock Cycle/Rocks, Planetary Winds, Tectonic Plates, Radioactive Decay, Geologic Time, Luminosity/H-R, and the EM Spectrum come up most. Most data is printed in the booklet, so reading the stimulus matters more than memorizing.
- **The exam tests skills, not recall.** Many MC choices are tables ("Which row correctly pairs the claim with the evidence?") or cause-and-effect chains.
- **Human impact and design solutions are about a third of the exam.** These are the ESS3 standards: judging dams, mining, carbon capture, hazard plans, and similar solutions against criteria and constraints.

- **ESS2-3 and ESS3-3 are rarely the main focus of an item**, but students need them as background (e.g., interior models in plate-boundary items).
- **ESS1-7 and ESS2-8 are NYS-added** and live mostly in the optional Seasons and Moon Phases chapters and in Unit 5. Aug 2026 had **6 Earth-Sun-Moon items**, so don't skip them.

## 3. Skills to track

### A. Science practices (SEPs), weighted by the Regents

| Code | Skill | What it looks like | % of Regents items |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEP4 | **Analyze & interpret data** | Read graphs, tables, and maps; describe a trend; compare two scenarios or time periods | 28% |
| SEP2 | **Develop & use models** | Read, complete, label, or put in order a cross-section, flow diagram, H-R diagram, or orbit model | 26% |
| SEP6 | **Explain / design solutions** | Explain a cause; choose or judge a solution using criteria, constraints, and trade-offs | 24% |
| SEP7 | **Argue from evidence** | Choose or write the evidence that supports or refutes a claim; pick the matching claim + evidence row | 14% |
| SEP5 | **Math & computational thinking** | Rate, percent change, eccentricity, elapsed time, unit conversion, reading values off a graph | 6% |
| SEP8 | **Read & evaluate information** | Pull key facts from a dense passage or infographic (*every* cluster depends on this, even when it isn't the main skill) | 2% |
| SEP3 | Plan & carry out investigations | Variables, controls, repeat trials. Not on these written exams, but central to packets and labs | 0% |
| SEP1 | Ask questions / define problems | Testable questions; criteria and constraints. Comes up in packets and performance tasks | 0% |

### B. Regents test-taking skills (also tracked in the spreadsheet)

| Skill | Why track it | Share of Regents |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference Tables fluency** | Finding the right table fast, then reading it correctly | 21% of items |
| **Constructed response** | Complete 1-credit answers: *identify, then explain*, quoting numbers from the data | 37% of items |

### C. Crosscutting concepts (the "lens" the question asks for)

Cause & Effect (36% of items) · Patterns (20%) · Energy & Matter (12%) · Systems & System Models (12%) · Stability & Change (10%) · Scale, Proportion & Quantity (8%) · Structure & Function (3%).

*Tip:* "Cause & Effect" plus "Patterns" covers more than half the exam. A sentence frame like *"As ___ increases, ___ decreases because ___"* pays off across every unit.

### D. Recurring "Regents moves" worth explicit practice

1. **Pick the row.** Match a claim to the evidence that supports it in a 2–3 column table.
2. **Direction of change.** Read a graph and state how one variable changes as another changes, with numbers.
3. **Compare two scenarios.** Before/after, two locations, or two solutions.
4. **Put a model in order.** Sequence steps (subduction, carbon pathway, energy from core to surface).
5. **Judge a solution.** Which option best meets the criteria? What is one trade-off?
6. **Systems chain.** How a change in one sphere ripples into another (oxygen and banded iron, ice and albedo, CO₂ and temperature).
7. **Extend a cycle.** Predict the next tide, eclipse, or orbital position from a repeating pattern.

## 4. Ways to track 100 students (from least to most effort)

### Option 1: Item-tagged spreadsheet *(recommended; template included)*
Every question you give is tagged once with a standard and a skill. You enter each student's points per question, and the sheet calculates each student's percent on every standard and every skill.

- **Why it scales:** you score each assessment once, and every standard and skill updates from that. With Google Forms quizzes, export the 0/1 scores and paste them in, which takes about 5 minutes per assessment for 100 students.
- **Past Regents are already mapped.** Any cluster from the five exams can be a do-now, quiz, or homework. Type the Item ID (e.g., `Jun2026-Q14`) in the Scores header and the tags fill in.
- **What you get:** red/yellow/green grids by student, class averages, "# of students below 50%" per standard (your reteach list), and a one-page **Student Report** for conferences or tutoring groups.
- **Packet tasks and performance tasks:** add a row with Points = 4 and enter the rubric score.

### Option 2: Unit checkpoints built from past-Regents clusters
At the end of each unit, give 2–3 real Regents clusters (10–15 items, mostly 1-credit, about 20 min) that match that unit's standards. Sort `regents-item-map.csv` by `pe` to pick them. Then:
- Do a **mid-year mock** (half an exam) in late January and a **full mock** in April/May.
- After each checkpoint, use the "# students < 50%" row to form 3–4 small groups by standard or skill for reteaching and tutoring.
- This gives a consistent Regents-readiness trend line across the year.

### Option 3: Student self-tracking (shares the work with students)
Give each student a one-page tracker in their packet listing the 21 "I can" statements and the 8 skills. After each checkpoint they color each one (red/yellow/green) from their returned scores and write one goal. This builds metacognition and costs you no data entry; spot-check during conferences. It works well **alongside** Option 1.

### Option 4: Skills rubric on constructed responses
Students' written and modeled answers are where skills are visible. Pick one CR per week and score it on a 4-point scale for one skill only (e.g., "used numbers from the data as evidence"). Enter it in the tracker as an item with Points = 4. Rotate the skill each week so all eight get covered over a marking period.

### Option 5 (bigger lift): Extend the textbook dashboards
The textbook's end-of-chapter quizzes could report scores by standard into a dashboard like the Unit 1 one in `teacher/`, using the optional backend. Every quiz question would then need a standard tag, and you'd need a privacy-safe way to identify students. Worth discussing later if Options 1–3 prove useful.

**Suggested combination:** Option 1 as the gradebook, Option 2 for the checkpoints, and Option 3 so students own their progress.

## 5. Using the spreadsheet: quick start

1. Upload `ESS-progress-tracker.xlsx` to Google Drive (File → Import), or open it in Excel.
2. **Scores** sheet: paste student IDs, names, and periods into rows 5–104.
3. **Item Map** sheet: the 250 Regents items are already there. Add a row for each of your own questions (Item ID, PE, skill, points).
4. **Scores** row 1: type the Item IDs for an assessment. Rows 2–4 fill in automatically (a cell showing "not in map" means the ID is missing from Item Map). Enter points earned, and leave a cell blank if the student was absent.
5. Check **Standards Mastery**, **Skills Mastery**, and **Student Report**.

### Paper tests

For tests given on paper, use a **paper-entry sheet**. The first one, `U2 Mid-Unit 1` (Unit 2 Mid-Unit Task 1, *Giant Insects*), is already built:

1. Type the multiple-choice answer key once in the yellow row, using the Rating Guide. The key is left blank in this file on purpose: the repo is public, and students use the site.
2. Holding each student's test, type the **choice number they circled** (1–4) for each MC question and the **credit you gave** (0/1) for each written question. Press Tab between cells and Enter at the end of a row. It takes about 15–20 seconds per student.
3. The sheet grades the MC answers, totals each test, and fills the `Scores` sheet, so the standards and skills grids update right away.
4. The **Item Analysis** below the roster shows how many students picked each choice, which points to shared misconceptions.

The Giant Insects items follow New Visions' own alignment guide. All six assess **HS-ESS2-7** and **Stability & Change**. Q1 is analyzing data, Q3 is using models, and Q2 and Q4–Q6 are argument from evidence.

For a new paper test, duplicate the sheet and its `ZG` tab, change the Item IDs and the key, add the items to `Item Map`, and link the next empty `Scores` columns. You can also type 0/1 scores straight into `Scores`.

### Paper tests scanned with ZipGrade

Each entry sheet has a matching **`ZG` tab** (e.g., `U2 Mid-Unit 1 ZG`) where you paste a ZipGrade export. The sheet matches students **by ID**, so the order of the export and absent students don't matter.

1. **IDs:** the `Student ID` column in `Scores` must hold the same numbers students bubble on ZipGrade answer sheets. ZipGrade's roster import makes this easy to keep in sync.
2. **In ZipGrade:** make a quiz with the **MC questions only** (Q1–Q3 for Giant Insects), key 1→A, 2→B, 3→C, 4→D, and scan the sheets.
3. **Export** the quiz results as a CSV, open it, select all, copy, and paste into the `ZG` tab at **cell A1**.
4. **First time only:** check the green cells on the entry sheet.
   - **B6** must match the export's student-ID column header (default `ZipGradeID`). C6 shows a number when it is found.
   - **Row 7** must match each question's *student answer* column header (default `Stu1`, `Stu2`, `Stu3`).
   - If your export names them differently, type the real header names in.
   - The "In ZipGrade?" column then shows ✓ for every matched student.
5. **Type the written-answer credits (Q4–Q6) yourself.** Anything typed on the entry sheet overrides ZipGrade, which also lets you fix a misread bubble.

Letters (A–D, either case) and numbers both work.

Colors: red < 50% · yellow 50–74% · green ≥ 75%. Percentages count only the items a student actually attempted. There's room for 300 item columns; for the next year or semester, start a new copy.
